The Shape of the Modern Pitch

Ball-flight geometry, not velocity and not spin rate, is the pitch property that carries information about outcomes. It is also the only one measured well enough to judge a pitcher on a small sample.

Scott Luntz · August 2026 Statcast / Baseball Savant Every result reproducible from public data DOI 10.5281/zenodo.22037431

The finding

4.5× Shape beats velocity. Movement and approach angle explain 4.5× more variation in run value than release speed does.
0.00 Spin adds nothing. Once velocity is partialled out, residual spin contributes nothing measurable, even given first claim on shared variance.
0.99 vs 0.20 Shape is measured; results are sampled. Split-half reliability of shape versus run value per pitch.

Why it changes a decision

Shape is a near-deterministic property of how a pitcher throws, so it is known almost exactly from the first pitch. Results are a sample, and at small volume they are mostly luck.

That creates a crossover. Below roughly 80 pitches of a given pitch type, a pitcher's ball flight predicts his next season better than his own results do. Above it, results win and keep pulling away.

The window below that line is exactly where evaluation is hardest and most expensive to get wrong: the callup, the waiver claim, the deadline target you have seen twice, the arm that just added a pitch.

What it does not claim

  • Command outranks shape ~50:1. Location is the dominant term in any pitch outcome. Shape sets the ceiling; command determines how much of it is reached.
  • Shape does not replace results on full seasons. At 500 pitches, past results predict next-season whiff rate at R² 0.362 against shape's 0.188.
  • The study rejected its own starting hypothesis — that spin had displaced velocity — and narrowed the surviving claim twice more. All three reversals are documented in the paper.

Also built

A leaderboard that flags regression and progression candidates from the gap between a pitcher's results and what his pitch shape supports, ranked by what shape adds beyond ordinary mean reversion.

Validated by walk-forward backtest across 1,413 out-of-sample pitcher-seasons, refitting on prior seasons only. Shape lifts R² over mean reversion in seven of eight seasons, and the top edge decile beats the bottom by 2.5 percentage points of realised whiff rate.

How to use it

Under ~80 pitches seen Read the shape. Discount the results. Rank IVB, height-adjusted VAA, and horizontal break against the league distribution for that pitch type. The outcome line is close to uninformative here.
~80 to 400 pitches Blend, and read the disagreement. Elite shape with poor results is usually a command or sequencing problem, which is coachable. Ordinary shape with strong results is the profile most likely to regress.
Full season and beyond Lead with results. Keep shape as the alarm. Because shape is measured exactly, a two-inch drop in IVB or a flattening release shows up in a single outing, long before results move.
